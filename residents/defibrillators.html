Local lifesaving equipment

Defibrillators in Highworth.

Know where to find an AED, and what to do if someone collapses. In an emergency, call 999 first.

If someone collapses and is unresponsive

Call 999 immediately

Check it is safe to approach. Shout for help and put your phone on speaker. The call handler will help you check breathing. If they are not breathing normally, start CPR. Gasping is not normal breathing.

On your own? Stay with the person and keep doing CPR. Ask someone else to fetch a defibrillator if possible; follow the call handler’s directions.

Based on Resuscitation Council UK’s public CPR guidance.

Start CPR while help is coming

These compression instructions are for an adult who is unresponsive and not breathing normally. Follow the 999 call handler’s instructions.

  1. Press in the centre of the chest

    Put the heel of one hand on the lower half of the breastbone, with your other hand on top and fingers interlocked. Keep your arms straight.

  2. Push hard and fast

    Aim for 100–120 compressions a minute, pressing down 5–6 cm. Let the chest come back up fully between pushes. Minimise interruptions.

  3. Keep going

    If trained to give rescue breaths, alternate 30 compressions with 2 breaths. Otherwise give continuous chest compressions. Continue until the AED tells you to pause, the ambulance team takes over, or the person shows signs of life and breathes normally. Tell the call handler about any change.

For a child or baby: call 999 and follow the operator’s age-specific CPR instructions; the adult compression depth above does not apply.

Sources: Resuscitation Council UK — 2025 adult basic life support guidelines and public CPR steps.

Follow the voice prompts

Using a defibrillator

An automated external defibrillator (AED) talks you through what to do. You do not need previous training.

  1. Switch it on

    Open the lid or press the power button, depending on the device. Follow its spoken and visual instructions.

  2. Attach the pads to bare skin

    Expose the chest and place the pads exactly where their diagrams show. If another helper is doing CPR, they should continue while you attach the pads.

  3. Stand clear when told

    Make sure nobody touches the person while the AED checks their heart rhythm or delivers a shock. Some devices shock automatically; others tell you to press a button. A shock is only advised if needed.

  4. Resume CPR immediately

    After a shock, or if no shock is advised, restart CPR as prompted. Leave the pads attached and keep following the AED and ambulance call handler.

Sources: British Heart Foundation — how to use a defibrillator and Resuscitation Council UK’s AED guidance.

Learn before you need it

Watch Resuscitation Council UK’s CPR demonstration and read the British Heart Foundation’s defibrillator guide. Familiarise yourself with the devices near home, work and the places you visit.

Look after a public defibrillator? Register it with The Circuit and keep its access and maintenance details up to date so ambulance services can find it.
